# DR-11 — Professional Music-Tool UX and Progressive Disclosure

**Current date:** 2026-08-14  
**Program:** Aural Geometry Lab

## Mission

Produce an evidence-grounded interaction-design dossier for a mathematical music studio that must work for curious newcomers without capping expert composition workflows.

Research current and historically influential interaction patterns from DAWs, modular/node systems, live-coding systems, generative music tools, mathematical visualization tools, notation software, and iPad creative applications. The goal is not to copy a DAW; it is to identify which interaction conventions users already understand, which create discoverability problems, and how AGL should bridge **Explore → Compose → Inspect**.

## Questions

1. Which layout and interaction patterns recur in professional music creation tools, and which are contingent on legacy hardware metaphors?
2. How do successful tools manage dense timeline + inspector + mixer + browser + graph workflows?
3. What node-graph UX patterns reduce connection errors and navigation burden?
4. How do direct-manipulation creative tools expose numeric precision without undermining tactile interaction?
5. How do iPad music/creative tools adapt professional density to touch, Pencil, keyboard, and pointer?
6. Which onboarding/progressive-disclosure methods preserve an expert ceiling?
7. How should generated/procedural content be visually distinguished from committed/editable material?
8. How do tools communicate non-destructive transformations and provenance?
9. What failure patterns should AGL explicitly avoid?
10. Which findings materially require changes to `docs/13-ui-ux-final-design-spec.md`?

## Comparative sample

Include, where evidence is available, representatives from:

- Ableton Live;
- Logic Pro, including iPad;
- Bitwig Studio;
- Max/MSP;
- TouchDesigner or comparable node-based creative environment;
- modular synthesis interfaces;
- notation/composition tools;
- Strudel/Tidal/SuperCollider-style algorithmic systems;
- OpenMusic/Opusmodus or similar algorithmic-composition environments;
- at least two modern iPad-native creative/pro audio applications;
- at least one accessibility-oriented creative tool.

Do not confuse popularity with empirical usability evidence.

## Required outputs

1. Pattern taxonomy with screenshots/diagrams cited where licensing permits description.
2. Comparative matrix: discoverability, density, precision, direct manipulation, keyboard, touch, graph, generated-content semantics, accessibility.
3. 15–25 design rules specifically for AGL.
4. Anti-pattern catalog.
5. Recommended desktop/iPad layout options with trade-offs.
6. Suggested usability-test tasks and success metrics.
7. Changes proposed to design tokens/components/screens.
8. Claim/evidence table with dates and source quality.
9. ADR proposal for the AGL interaction shell.

## Evidence requirements

Prioritize:

- official product documentation and design guidance;
- primary HCI research;
- peer-reviewed papers;
- published accessibility evaluations;
- credible practitioner studies for professional creative tools.

Separate observed product behavior from empirically validated usability claims.
