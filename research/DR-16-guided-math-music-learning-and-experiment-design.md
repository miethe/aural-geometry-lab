# DR-16 — Guided Math–Music Learning and Experiment Design

**Current date:** 2026-08-14  
**Program:** Aural Geometry Lab

## Mission

Turn each lab into a rigorous but engaging guided experiment without compromising the product's role as a creative instrument.

## Questions

1. Which sequencing of hear → manipulate → predict → compare → explain best supports learning in interactive math/music contexts?
2. How much mathematical notation should appear at each depth level?
3. How should AGL distinguish perceptual reports (“I hear acceleration”) from mathematical facts and scientific claims?
4. What scaffolding helps users understand mappings without confusing correlation with causation?
5. How should misconceptions be surfaced and corrected without breaking exploratory flow?
6. What forms of prediction/observation capture improve learning without making the product feel like schoolwork?
7. How should guided experiments adapt for children, adult hobbyists, musicians, and mathematically sophisticated users?
8. What metrics can evaluate conceptual learning and creative agency?
9. How should explanation depth and terminology be personalized without changing project semantics?
10. What is the minimum high-quality guided experiment set per MVP lab?

## Required outputs

- learning-design principles;
- 3–5 complete guided experiment scripts per lab;
- misconception catalog;
- prediction/observation UI patterns;
- glossary hierarchy;
- age/expertise adaptation guidance;
- evaluation protocol;
- evidence/claim tags for each experiment;
- changes to lab acceptance criteria and UX copy.

## Evidence requirements

Prioritize peer-reviewed learning science, multimedia learning, embodied/direct-manipulation learning, auditory perception education, and primary math/music education research. Avoid unsupported “learning styles” claims.
